Four people, one bench

Everything we sell is made here, by hand, from recycled metal. This page shows how, step by step, with the hours it takes.

The bench · how a ring gets made
Hours at the bench00:00
01

Recycled gold, melted down

We start with casting grain refined from old jewellery. It is weighed to a tenth of a gram and melted in a graphite crucible.

Recycled 18k grain · 6.4 g · 1,064 °C
02

Bent round and soldered shut

The ingot is rolled into wire, bent round a steel mandrel to your size and soldered at the seam. Then it is hammered true.

Size N · steel mandrel · 18k hard solder
03

The stone goes in by hand

Four claws are raised, notched and pushed over the stone under a 10× loupe. It takes a steady hour and a steadier hand.

10× loupe · 4 claws · 1.02 ct
04

Six grades of polish

From coarse emery to rouge on a cotton mop, every surface is taken back until the reflection is clean, inside and out.

Emery → tripoli → rouge · 6 passes
05

Tested, then stamped

Every piece goes to the assay office. Once the metal is proven, it is stamped inside: 750 for 18k gold, then our maker's mark.

Assay office · 750 · maker's mark AS

The tools

Old tools, used every day

Some of these are older than we are. They were bought from retiring jewellers.

Some of these are older than we are. They were bought from retiring jewellers.

01

Torch and block

A small gas torch and a charcoal block for melting, annealing and soldering.

02

Mandrel and hammers

A steel ring mandrel and rawhide hammers to bend and true every band.

03

Loupe and gravers

A 10× loupe and hand-sharpened gravers for raising claws and setting stones.

04

Polishing mop

Six grades of compound, from emery to rouge, on cotton and felt mops.

Where it comes from

Recycled metal, traceable stones

Our gold and silver is refined from old jewellery and trade scrap. Diamonds are lab-grown or recut antiques; coloured stones come from named dealers, and the origin goes on your certificate.
